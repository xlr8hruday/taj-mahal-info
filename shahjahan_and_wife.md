# Shah Jahan and Mumtaz Mahal

**Shah Jahan** (1592‑1666) was the fifth Mughal emperor of India, best known for commissioning the Taj Mahal as a mausoleum for his beloved wife.

**Mumtaz Mahal** (born Arjumand Banu Begum, 1593‑1631) was Shah Jahan’s favourite consort. Their love story is legendary; she accompanied him on many campaigns and was his trusted confidante.

The Taj Mahal was built between 1632 and 1653 in Agra, near the Yamuna River, as a symbol of Shah Jahan’s devotion to Mumtaz. After her death during childbirth, he ordered the grand marble complex to be erected, which today stands as a UNESCO World Heritage site and an enduring testament to their love.

*Key points*
- Shah Jahan’s reign marked a golden age of Mughal architecture.
- Mumtaz Mahal’s name means “the exalted one of the palace”.
- Their partnership influenced art, poetry, and the empire’s cultural flourishing.
